# Theorem NS-I.3 — Geometric Dissipation Lemma

**Status:** PROVED analytically under the hypotheses below  
**Track:** Navier–Stokes, Part I

## Hypotheses and domain

Let \(u(x,t)\) be a sufficiently smooth solution of the unforced three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with kinematic viscosity \(\nu>0\). Let
\[
\omega=\nabla\times u,\qquad \rho=|\omega|,\qquad \mathcal R_t=\{x:\rho(x,t)>0\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal R_t\), define \(\xi=\omega/\rho\), so \(\omega=\rho\xi\) and \(|\xi|=1\). All identities below are local on this regular set and wherever the displayed derivatives exist.

## Lemma

On \(\mathcal R_t\),
\[
\xi\cdot\Delta\omega=\Delta\rho-\rho|\nabla\xi|^2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\nu\,\xi\cdot\Delta\omega=\nu\Delta\rho-\nu\rho|\nabla\xi|^2.
\]
Define \(N:=\nu\rho|\nabla\xi|^2\). Then \(N\ge0\) pointwise on \(\mathcal R_t\).

## Proof

The product rule gives
\[
\Delta(\rho\xi)=(\Delta\rho)\xi+
2\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_j\rho)(\partial_j\xi)+\rho\Delta\xi.
\]
Since \(|\xi|^2=1\), differentiation yields \(\xi\cdot\partial_j\xi=0\). Applying the Laplacian to \(\xi\cdot\xi=1\) gives
\[
0=2|\nabla\xi|^2+2\xi\cdot\Delta\xi,
\qquad \xi\cdot\Delta\xi=-|\nabla\xi|^2.
\]
Contract the product-rule identity with \(\xi\). The cross term vanishes, proving
\[
\xi\cdot\Delta\omega=\Delta\rho-\rho|\nabla\xi|^2.
\]
Finally, \(\nu>0\), \(\rho>0\) on \(\mathcal R_t\), and \(|\nabla\xi|^2\ge0\), so \(N\ge0\). \(\square\)

## Interpretation and limits

The term \(N\) is the nonnegative geometric contribution generated by spatial variation of the unit vorticity direction. It is not an additional physical viscosity; the original viscous operator remains \(\nu\Delta\omega\).

This local identity does not prove that \(N\) dominates vortex stretching, does not provide a global-in-time bound by itself, and does not establish global regularity. The set \(\rho=0\) is outside the definition of \(\xi\) and requires separate treatment.
