**Author / Researcher:** Philippe Beauchamp  
**ORCID:** [0009-0003-7407-394X](https://orcid.org/0009-0003-7407-394X)

> **Status:** Public mathematical certificate / structural-arithmetic checkpoint.  
> This document records the index-2 extension used in the lattice calculation. It does **not** claim a proof that the resulting lattice is the full geometric lattice of algebraic cycles.

## 1. Purpose

The preceding SNF certificate records an explicit rank-two lattice and its discriminant group. The next structural issue is whether the initially generated lattice is already saturated, or whether it admits a finite-index enlargement inside the intended ambient integral lattice.

The concrete operation considered here is the index-2 extension

$$
R = \frac{P+Q}{2}.
$$

The point of this certificate is to isolate that operation as an arithmetic object and to keep the following questions separate:

- **index:** what finite index is introduced by adjoining (R)?
- **integrality:** does the enlarged lattice remain integral for the specified pairing?
- **saturation:** is the enlarged lattice saturated in the stated ambient lattice?
- **geometric realization:** does that ambient lattice coincide with the intended lattice of algebraic cycles?

Only the first three are lattice-theoretic questions; the last requires additional geometric input.

## 2. The index-2 extension

Let

$$
L_0 = \mathbf Z P \oplus \mathbf Z Q
$$

be the lattice generated by the two displayed classes.

Adjoin

$$
R=\frac{P+Q}{2}.
$$

Then

$$
2R=P+Q\in L_0,
$$

while (R) is not in (L_0) when (P+Q) is not divisible by (2) in (L_0).

Therefore the quotient of the enlarged lattice

$$
L_1=L_0+\mathbf ZR
$$

by (L_0) is cyclic of order (2):

$$
L_1/L_0\cong\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z.
$$

Hence

$$
[L_1:L_0]=2.
$$

This is the precise arithmetic meaning of the index-2 extension.

## 3. Coordinate/SNF checkpoint

Relative to the ordered generators ((P,Q)), the new generator is

$$
R=\frac12P+\frac12Q.
$$

Equivalently, after choosing an integral basis adapted to the extension, the finite-index relation is detected by a Smith-normal-form invariant

$$
\operatorname{SNF}=\operatorname{diag}(1,2).
$$

Thus the extension has exactly one nontrivial elementary divisor, equal to (2).

This is a statement about the finite-index lattice extension. It should not be confused with the SNF of a Gram matrix, which records a different invariant.

## 4. Gram/discriminant checkpoint for the saturated candidate

For the candidate lattice obtained after the index-2 extension and the subsequent saturation calculation, the recorded Gram determinant is

$$
\det(G_{\mathrm{sat}})=-144.
$$

Consequently the discriminant has absolute order

$$
|\det(G_{\mathrm{sat}})|=144.
$$

In the recorded rank-two model, the resulting abstract discriminant group is

$$
A_{\mathrm{sat}}
\cong
\mathbf Z/12\mathbf Z\oplus\mathbf Z/12\mathbf Z.
$$

This statement is an arithmetic identification of the specified candidate lattice. It does not, by itself, prove that this candidate is the complete geometric cycle lattice.

## 5. What “saturated” means here

Let (L_{\mathrm{amb}}) be the specified ambient integral lattice and (L\subseteq L_{\mathrm{amb}}) a sublattice.

The saturation is

$$
\operatorname{Sat}_{L_{\mathrm{amb}}}(L)
=
(L\otimes\mathbf Q)\cap L_{\mathrm{amb}}.
$$

The inclusion is saturated exactly when

$$
\operatorname{Sat}_{L_{\mathrm{amb}}}(L)=L.
$$

Thus an index-2 enlargement such as (L_0\subset L_1) is one concrete candidate step toward saturation, but it is not logically equivalent to proving full saturation in the ambient cohomology lattice.

A complete saturation proof requires the ambient lattice to be specified and the quotient

$$
\operatorname{Sat}_{L_{\mathrm{amb}}}(L)/L
$$

to be determined.

For an independently reproducible version of this certificate, record:

1. the exact ambient integral lattice (L_{\mathrm{amb}});
2. the ordered integral generators (P,Q);
3. the proof that (R=(P+Q)/2) belongs to the intended rational span;
4. the proof that (R\notin L_0), giving index (2);
5. the integral Gram matrix after adjoining (R);
6. the saturation quotient in the ambient lattice;
7. the resulting Gram determinant and discriminant group.

The present certificate records items 2–4 and the downstream arithmetic checkpoint in item 7, while explicitly leaving the ambient-geometric identification separate.
