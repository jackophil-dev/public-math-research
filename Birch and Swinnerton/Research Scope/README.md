## Scope

This research program investigates the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture through elliptic-curve arithmetic, analytic rank, real periods, regulators, local factors, torsion, the Tate–Shafarevich group, integral-lattice methods, and certificate-based computational protocols. A supplementary Ammonite-inspired geometric model explores logarithmic-spiral kinematics and a proposed geometry-to-arithmetic connection.

The existence of a geometric identity or a valid dynamical derivation does not itself establish a correspondence with an elliptic curve's L-function or arithmetic invariants. Any such bridge requires an explicitly defined map, stated hypotheses, and a proof of its relevant properties.

## Evidence categories

- **PROVED:** a mathematical statement established under its explicit hypotheses and scope.
- **COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED:** a result supported by a specified computation and its verification conditions.
- **DERIVED:** a consequence of stated assumptions, definitions, or established results.
- **OPEN:** a claim, connection, or verification task not established by the current record.

## Principal open obligations

- Recover and bind the original curve-specific input and parameters.
- Identify the elliptic curve and verify the relevant arithmetic data.
- Verify independent rational-point generators, rank, and regulator.
- Complete analytic calculations and real-period normalization under explicit conventions.
- Verify local factors, torsion, and any Tate–Shafarevich order claims.
- Establish or reject rigorously the proposed Ammonite-to-arithmetic bridge.
- Check each theorem and certificate against its complete statement, hypotheses, and evidence.

The standard BSD leading-term formula is recorded as the conjectural reference framework, not as a proof of the general conjecture. The release preserves unresolved obligations rather than presenting them as completed.
