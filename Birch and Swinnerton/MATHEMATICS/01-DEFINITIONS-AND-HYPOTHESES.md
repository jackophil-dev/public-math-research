# BSD — Definitions and Hypotheses

Let \(E/\mathbb Q\) be an elliptic curve of conductor \(N\), with Hasse–Weil L-function \(L(E,s)\). Mordell–Weil gives \(E(\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Z^r\oplus E(\mathbb Q)_{tors}\); \(r\) is the algebraic rank.

BSD predicts that the analytic order of vanishing \(r_{an}=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\) equals \(r\), and gives a refined leading-term relation. The period \(\Omega_E\), regulator, Tamagawa numbers, and L-function normalization depend on conventions. A formula must state the model, differential, period convention, and normalization.

The Tate–Shafarevich group \(\Sha(E/\mathbb Q)\) is conjecturally finite in the classical leading-term formulation; its order cannot be assumed known for arbitrary curves.

Any curve-specific computation requires an explicit curve equation and input provenance. The public record does not provide a recovered original curve/parameter set, so none is invented here.
